Assume that we are only able to move right and down. In a $2\times2$ grid, to get from the top left to the bottom right we need to make 2 right moves and 2 down moves. Once we choose the down moves (resp. right moves) the right moves (resp. down moves) are automatically chosen. So from the $4$ total moves we only have to choose $2$. There are 
$${4 \choose2} = 6 \text{ ways to do it}  $$
Thus, $6$ possible routes.

\vspace{0.5cm}

For a $n \times n$ grid, we need a total of $2n$ moves, $n$ right and $n$ down. Again, the number of possible paths equals the number of possible choises of the down (or the right) moves. Thus, there are 
$${2n \choose n} \text{ possible paths}.$$

\vspace{0.5cm}

For the $20 \times 20$ grid case, the number of possible routes is
\begin{align*}
    \boxed{{40 \choose20}=137846528820}
\end{align*}
