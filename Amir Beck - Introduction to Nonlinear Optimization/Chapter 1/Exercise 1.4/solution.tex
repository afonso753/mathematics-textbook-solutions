$\mathbf{1.4.}$ \textbf{Prove the Cauchy Schwarz inequality. Show that equality holds if and only if the vectors x and y are lienarly dependent.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

Suppose $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ are linearly dependent, that is, there exists a scalar $c \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $\mathbf{x}=c\mathbf{y}$, then
\begin{align*}
    |<\mathbf{x,\mathbf{y}}>| &= |<c \mathbf{y}, \mathbf{y}>|\\
    &=|c<\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{y}>|\\
    &=|c|.||\mathbf{y||^2}\\
    &= (|c|||\mathbf{y}||)||\mathbf{y}||\\
    &= ||c \mathbf{y||.||y||}\\
    &=||\mathbf{x}||.||\mathbf{y||}
\end{align*}

Suppose that, without loss of generality, $\mathbf{x}=0$. Then $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ are linearly dependent and by the previous argument the (in)equality is satisfied.

\vspace{0.2cm}

We now suppose that both vectors are nonzero. Define $p: \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ such that 
\begin{align*}
    p(t) :& =||\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y}||^2\\
          & =<\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y}>\\
          & = <\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}>+2t<\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}>+t^2<\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}>\\
          & = ||\mathbf{x}||^2+2t<\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}>+t^2||\mathbf{y}||^2
\end{align*}

Since $\mathbf{y} \neq 0$, $||\mathbf{y}|| \neq 0$ and thus, $p$ is a polynomial of degree 2.

By the positive definiteness property of the inner product, it must be the case that $p(t) \geq 0$, for any $t \in \mathbb{R}$. That implies that
\begin{align*}
    \Delta=(2<\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}>)^2&-4||\mathbf{x}||^2.||\mathbf{y}||^2 \leq 0\\
    &\Leftrightarrow\\
    <\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}>^2 &\leq ||\mathbf{x}||^2.||\mathbf{y}||^2
\end{align*}

and the result follows by taking square roots on both sides of the previous inequality.

Finally, for the equality to happen, we need 
\begin{align*}
    0=\Delta\; \Leftrightarrow\; <\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}>^2 = ||\mathbf{x}||^2.||\mathbf{y}||^2
\end{align*}
In this case, the only zero of $p$ is given by
\begin{align*}
    t_0=\frac{-2<\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}>}{2||\mathbf{y}||^2}=\frac{-<\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}>}{||\mathbf{y}||^2} \in \mathbb{R}.
\end{align*}

Furthermore,
\begin{align*}
    0=p(t_0)=<\mathbf{x}+t_0\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}+t_0\mathbf{y}>
\end{align*}

But by the positive definiteness property of the inner product we have that
\begin{align*}
    0=\mathbf{x}+t_0\mathbf{y}
    \Leftrightarrow
    \mathbf{x}=-t_0 \mathbf{y}
\end{align*}

Therefore, $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ are linearly dependent.
