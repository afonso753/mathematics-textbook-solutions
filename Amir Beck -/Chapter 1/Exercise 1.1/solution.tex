$\mathbf{1.1}$ \textbf{Show that} $\mathbf{||.||_\frac{1}{2}}$ \textbf{is not a norm.}

By definition, for any $\mathbf{x}=(x_1,...,x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$
\begin{align*}
    ||\mathbf{x}||_{1/2}=\left( \sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|^{1/2} \right)^2.
\end{align*}

Take $n=2$ and $\mathbf{x}=(1,1), \mathbf{y}=(1,0) \in \mathbb{R}^2$. We have that
$$||\mathbf{x}+ \mathbf{y}||_{1/2}=(\sqrt{|1+1|}+\sqrt{|1+0|})^2=(\sqrt{2}+1)^2=3+2\sqrt{2}>5$$
and
\begin{align*}
    ||\mathbf{x}||_{1/2}+||\mathbf{y}||_{1/2}=(\sqrt{|1|}+\sqrt{|1|})^2+(\sqrt{|1|}+\sqrt{|0|})^2=4+1=5
\end{align*}

That is,

\begin{align*}
    ||\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}||_{1/2}=3+2\sqrt{2}>5=||\mathbf{x}||_{1/2}+||\mathbf{y}||_{1/2}
\end{align*}
which means that $||.||_{1/2}$ does not satisfy the triangle innequality. Therefore $||.||_{1/2}$ is not a norm.
